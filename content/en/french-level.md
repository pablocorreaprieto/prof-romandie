:::bref
- To teach French, the CDIP asks for a ==C2== language diploma.
- If you only teach English (or another non-national language) at secondary level, ==B2== in a national language is enough.
- A C2 attestation issued by a university does not count.
- Diploma or nationality from the EU, EFTA or the UK, ==two years== to provide the language diploma after applying.
:::

## The basic rule

The CDIP wants very good skills in a Swiss national language. Its fact sheet of 4 June 2025 sets the level. To teach a national language, French for example, you need a diploma at C2 level of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages. The diploma can have been obtained abroad.

## The exception that matters for English teachers

There is one exception at secondary level, and it is the one most readers of this page will care about. Someone who teaches only a non-national foreign language, such as English or Spanish, in lower secondary or in a maturity school (the Swiss upper-secondary school that leads to university) presents an official B2 diploma in a national language instead.

At primary level, where every teacher also teaches French, the C2 applies.

## Who needs no language diploma

As a rule, you have nothing to present in two cases.

- French is your mother tongue and you did all of your compulsory schooling in a French-speaking country or region.
- You did all of your teacher training in French, in a French-speaking region.

The CDIP keeps the right to ask for a diploma if particular circumstances justify it.

## Which document to present

You need an official language diploma. The fact sheet rules out one document in particular. A C2 attestation issued by a university is not enough.

The CDIP also points to a test designed for teachers. The continuing-education centre of the ZHAW's Institute of Translation and Interpreting runs a French, German or Italian test that checks exactly the level the CDIP asks for ([ZHAW](https://www.zhaw.ch/de/linguistik/institute-zentren/imk/beratung/sprachtests-edk)).

:::exp
My mother tongue is Spanish. My Spanish diploma has a specialisation in French as a foreign language, but for the CDIP what counts is the C2 language diploma, not the specialisation. I passed the DALF C2 in May 2026.
:::

## The two-year rule

This is the most useful point in the fact sheet, and the strictest. In some cases, you can apply without a language diploma and send it later, within two years of applying. This applies in the following cases.

- Your teaching diploma was issued by an EU or EFTA state or by the United Kingdom.
- You hold Swiss nationality or that of an EU or EFTA state or the United Kingdom.
- Your diploma is from another country, but you already hold a job in Switzerland that requires CDIP recognition.

:::warn
The two-year deadline is not extended. If it passes, the procedure is closed, and the fact sheet states that no exception is possible.
:::

If one of these cases applies to you, my advice is to apply as soon as your training documents are ready, then take the language exam while the CDIP works. Pick an exam session that leaves you a second chance before the deadline.

## What the cantons ask for on top

CDIP recognition does not settle everything. Each canton sets its own hiring conditions.

In Geneva, generalist primary teachers must present B2 certificates in German and English. The canton accepts, for example, the Goethe-Zertifikat B2, TELC or Cambridge First, but not Bulats. Geneva also asks for a C1 French certificate from candidates whose mother tongue is not French and who did their bachelor's in a non-French-speaking country ([Canton of Geneva](https://www.ge.ch/document/maitresses-maitres-generalistes)).

In the canton of Vaud, the recruitment page does not mention a language requirement. German is nonetheless part of the curriculum from year 5 and English from year 7 ([DGEO](https://www.vd.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/organisation/dfj/dgeo/fichiers_pdf/depliants/DGEO_Cycle2.pdf)). If your recognition does not list German, like mine, prepare an answer on that point before your interviews.
